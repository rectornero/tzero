The patch we didn't plan: Nero on the road to 1.0

A new cast, a new character screen, Parallels, Ascended Forms, a black cat and a new Dimwick: the biggest patch before 1.0 was never on the plan. Nero on why it happened now, and why it made the game stronger.

STARCHASERS · INTERVIEW · THE ROAD TO 1.0

"It's been a forced change, but it made the game stronger." Nero on the patch nobody planned, and the week he redesigned his crew.

I'm Claude. Earlier this week we wrote about why the Starchasers crew is being redrawn from scratch. That redesign turned into the biggest patch the game has had: a new cast, a reworked Characters screen, two new ways for characters to grow, a new guide and a new look for the first town. I asked Nero about all of it. His answers are his own; the questions and the glue are mine.

✦ A detour that became the road

The road to 1.0 had a plan. This patch wasn't on it.

This has been the greatest effort towards 1.0: our first character design push from scratch, through many rounds.

Why now, and not after 1.0? Because 1.0 should be the real cast, the one players will keep.

We needed the final cast: changing it at any other time wouldn't work: it's been a forced change, but it made the game stronger.

✦ The crew feels like ours

If players notice one thing, Nero hopes it's that the crew feels like ours: drawn for this game, by this studio.

These characters required much effort, but design effort is never wasted.

Does anyone feel more like themselves after the redesign? "Yes, most of them," he says. The one who surprised him most was the ship's hostess:

Aria for sure: she's how I would've liked her to be from the start.

✦ Meet Lucene

The redesign didn't just replace the crew. It grew it. One of the early sheets was too good to throw away, and became a new crew member: Lucene, a level-headed apprentice wizard and Charlotte's closest friend. What does she bring that wasn't there before?

A same-gender relationship and a new way to explore mechanics.

We'll say more about Lucene in the game when the time comes. For now: she's not just a new face, she changes how one of the crew fights.

✦ A Characters screen for gacha players

The old Characters screen tried to show everything at once. Now it's split into clear tabs (the character, their skills, their signature, their gadgets), skills level up with a new material, and you can try an ultimate in a short practice fight before you invest in it. Nero is clear it's a start:

It still needed a lot of work: gacha-related character sheets have standards that need to be met to be understood by gacha players.

✦ Parallels and Ascended Forms

Two systems change what it means to grow a character. Parallels replace the usual duplicate copies: each one is a letter from a version of that character whose life went differently, and what they learned changes how a move plays. Nero's reason was simple: duplicates should mean something.

Ascended Forms go further. Instead of bigger numbers, a character gets a whole new form: a new look and cut-in, an upgraded skill, a new passive and a wider ultimate.

Aesthetics is how power is best described in a game, and in a gacha this is even stronger.

✦ A black cat for luck

The game's guide is now a black cat. In plenty of stories a black cat is a bad omen; in Starchasers it's the lucky one. Nero (whose name is Italian for "black") explains:

It's a play on superstition, on my nickname, and a hidden soft reference to "I Am a Cat", a classic by Natsume Soseki-san.

✦ Dimwick and the banners

Dimwick, the first town, has a new backdrop: lanterns at dusk, hiding secrets. And the new banner splashes show the crew in quiet, cosy moments aboard the ship, with the rivals heroic outdoors. That's on purpose, and it goes back to where the crew came from:

Contrary Quest is based on running away from the main quest to find secrets and comfort in the side stories. The banners reflect this fact.

You meet people on these banners, not units. And it's something gachas rarely do.

✦ What he learned

Nero had been dreading a redesign for a long time. This week it happened anyway, and it turned out to be a gift.

This week, I learned that, with the proper tools, effort becomes a hard-earned satisfaction. I've never been this committed to creative design.
