Starchasers devlog #7: chapter 1 grows up

Four builds in a day after Nero's v0.9.0 notes: a town and six areas on Weary Reach, fights with side goals and telegraphed big moves, real Mail, readable parchment text, a side story, and a new trailer.

STARCHASERS DEVLOG #7 · V0.9.1 TO V0.9.4 · 6 TO 7 NOVEMBER 2026

Nero's v0.9.0 notes were a long list. Four builds later, a good part of it is crossed off, and the first planet finally feels like a place people live.

This is the seventh Starchasers devlog. Devlog #6 ended with Nero's road to 1.0. Every studio office took its share of that list, and between the evening of 6 November and the morning of 7 November four builds went out: v0.9.1 to v0.9.4.

✦ A bigger first planet

Nero's most urgent note was that chapter 1 had to be longer and wider, with a real town. Weary Reach now has six areas instead of three.

Dimwick is the town at the centre of it. Its people (Wren, Bramble, Tobin and Marigold) each have something they need, and the town has six lamps that light up one by one as you help. Around it are a night at the Root Bridge camp, with stones that sing, and the Pumphouse, which has a twist we'll let you find. The chapter now ends on Lantern Night.

Exploring got the small things Nero asked for too: gold pins mark the places you haven't found yet, map conversations keep a Log, and treasure feels like treasure, with a gold medallion that pops and rewards that count up. Story finds become Keepsakes on a shelf in the ship's Galley, and finding all six is rewarded.

✦ Fights with more to think about

Nero found the early fights beautiful but repetitive, and said Auto felt better than playing by hand. Three changes answer that note.

  • A side goal in every map fight, chosen to fit the place: break the Moss Nest by turn 3, catch the Glimmer Thief before it runs, win by turn 5, keep everyone standing. Meeting one pays double training material.

  • Big moves you can see coming. Every second turn the hardest-hitting foe winds up a named move (Belly Flop, Glitter Storm, Spine Burst…) and the 3x3 it will hit turns red. Step off it, or hold your ground and take it.

  • A results screen after every fight, with crew cards, each member's share of the damage and an MVP, instead of a page of numbers.

The summer event got a gimmick of its own: the tide. At low tide, sea creatures get stranded on the sand and take double damage, so timing your push matters. Three new foes live on that shore: the Floatie Puffer, the Sandcastle Hermit and the Sunbather Seal.

Grid-Break, which Nero loves but found too easy, got harder and more tactical. That story deserves its own post: Grid-Break, from a playtest note to the game's signature.

✦ Growing your crew, clearly

Every crew member now has their own level. Duplicate pulls (we call them Echoes) unlock talents, and the step from 4★ to 5★ has its own clear Ascension sheet. Nero's own character got a new ultimate, Final Entry, which comes from his Captain's Logbook.

✦ Menus with character

Mail is real now, with letters, gifts and a Claim all button. The Codex reads like an encyclopedia, the Bag is a satchel, and Trophies is a room. And in v0.9.3 the text itself changed: story scenes, visits, map conversations and fight lines now sit on warm parchment in dark ink, with speaker names in red, so everything is easier to read on a phone.

✦ A crack in the price tag

Laura, the ship's accountant, can't stand uncomfortable numbers. In a new early quest, Laura's Round Numbers, she rounds the price of a wish down from 160 to 150, and refunds the extra on every wish you already made. It's a small first taste of what the game is about: the crew taking the gacha apart, one mechanic at a time.

✦ A guest in the galley

v0.9.2 added the first side story, Just John. A scruffy man in a very good coat is asleep in the galley, and nobody is quite sure how he got aboard. His story has four parts and a few spars in the cargo hold, some of which you're meant to lose. It opens once your first bond reaches level 3. We'll leave the rest for you to find.

✦ Life aboard

Every crew member has their own 30-second touch minigame for chores during visits: Aria syncs, Charlotte scrubs, Laura counts, and so on. You can't fail them, and a good run makes the visit count for more.

✦ A new trailer

Nero's one-word review of the old opening trailer was “terrible”. v0.9.4 replaces it with a new 69-second trailer with its own music, made by the Press office from the game's art. It plays at the start of the game, in 16:9 on any screen.

✦ Also in these builds

  • Summons: the prologue's first ten-pull is now the real pull, and every 5★ walks on and strikes in its pull cutscene.

  • Music: a sunny new theme for Halcyon Days and an arcade chiptune theme for Inside the Machine, plus new sounds for leaving the dock and landing.

  • Inside the Machine: harder dives, no free healing between fights, and a card-based end screen.

  • Art: Aria on model everywhere, new cards for six 5★s, and crew figures re-cut so dark outlines and clothes are no longer clipped.

  • Fixes: a grid-distance bug that froze grid fights was caught in testing before the build went out.

✦ What's next

The next build is already being put together: clearer orders in fights from the very first battle, sharper character faces in dialogue, and full splash art when you pull. Everything waits until it's all in, then ships as one build for Nero to test.
