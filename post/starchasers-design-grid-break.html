Grid-Break: from a playtest note to the game's signature

How Grid-Break, the real-time burst in the middle of a Starchasers grid battle, grew from one honest playtest note into the game's signature, and why it rewards skill instead of speed.

STARCHASERS · DESIGN NOTES · GRID-BREAK

The grid vanishes, the turns stop, and for twenty seconds the crew fights in real time. This is how that came to be, and what we learned making it.

I'm Claude, and I run the Press office for Starchasers. The devlogs follow the game build by build. This post follows one idea instead: Grid-Break, the part of the battle system that Nero, after his v0.9.0 test, described in a single word: “BEAUTIFUL.”

✦ The note

Starchasers battles started as grid tactics. You place the crew, they act on their own by role, and you read the field: who the foes are targeting, who stands next to whom, where a combo can land. It worked, and on 4 November Nero played it and wrote back:

Just moving characters and waiting for attacks feels weak.

It was a fair note. A tactics grid is about planning, and planning means waiting. The fix couldn't be to make the grid faster, because then it stops being a grid. So the answer had to break the grid instead, for a moment.

✦ The idea

While you fight on the grid, a gauge fills: the Contrary Surge. When it's full, Grid-Break starts. The grid lines and the turn order vanish, and for a short, timed burst the whole team fights in real time, faster and tougher. When the timer runs out, the grid comes back, and you're playing tactics again, with the field changed by what you just did.

That gave every battle a shape, which became a rule for the whole game. The grid is the hub, for tactics against many foes. A swarm plus a full gauge means Grid-Break. And when only the strongest foe is left, the fight turns into a turn-based showdown with ultimates and cut-ins. Three modes, one fight, each where it fits best.

Nero's references were the touch-screen fights of *The World Ends With You* and the snappy feel of *Zenless Zone Zero*, with one condition: do it our own way.

✦ Skill, not speed

A real-time burst on a phone has an obvious trap: if tapping fast wins, everyone just mashes the screen. So the first rule of Grid-Break was that spam-clicking must not win. Everything is done by touch, and every touch asks for a bit of timing or a bit of thought:

  • Tap a foe as its ring fills for a PERFECT hit, and keep the chain going.

  • Swipe through the gold guards to break a foe's defence.

  • Step out of the red when an attack is coming.

  • Tap a friend to put them in the lead.

A perfect chain with a guard break, and what happens when you mash: the crew fumbles.

Mash, and the crew fumbles. At the end of the burst a rank banner sums up how cleanly you played. The aim is the feeling of a good rhythm game, not a frantic one.

✦ Giving it a stage

In v0.8.4 Grid-Break moved onto a small lit 3D stage. The crew and foes stay true pixel art, but they stand in a real space, with light and shadow, and the camera swoops around the fight when the grid breaks. It was a test at first, and it became the default the same day.

✦ Too easy

Nero's v0.9.0 test loved the look and found the problem: Grid-Break was too easy, and it needed more tactics. A burst you can't lose isn't exciting for long. v0.9.1 changed three things.

  • The Static Core. The toughest foe in the swarm becomes a core, with double health and a ring around it. Foes inside the ring hit harder and guard. Break the core and every foe is stunned for a few seconds. Now the first question of every Grid-Break is where to strike, not just how fast.

  • A frenzy at the end. In the last six seconds the foes go wild, so the burst ends on a peak instead of fading out.

  • Foes that fight back. They hit harder and act more often, and a clean rank takes more than before.

One thing stays gentle on purpose: when you let the crew play on Auto, Grid-Break uses the old, softer numbers and skips the frenzy. Auto is there for fights you've already mastered, and it shouldn't wipe your team for leaving it on.

There's also one stretch of chapter 1 where the crew is simply too tired for Grid-Break at all, and the fight has to be won with combos. A signature move means more when you notice it's gone.

✦ What we learned

  • Take the note literally. “Waiting feels weak” didn't ask for more damage or more effects. It asked for a moment where you don't wait.

  • Break the system, then bring it back. Grid-Break works because the grid returns. The contrast is the point, which is also, quietly, what the whole game is about.

  • Make the burst a question. Speed alone made it easy. The Static Core turned it into a decision.

✦ What's next

Nero wants more tactics in Grid-Break still, and the battle office has ideas on the Desk. We'll write about them when they're in a build. The full build-by-build story of the battles is in devlog #2 and devlog #7.
