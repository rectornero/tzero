Starchasers devlog #2: from grid tactics to Grid-Break

The first Starchasers battles: grid tactics, damage in the hundreds, ultimates with cut-ins, a real-time 1v1 duel, and Grid-Break, the game's signature.

STARCHASERS DEVLOG #2 · V0.3 TO V0.6 · 3 TO 4 NOVEMBER 2026

"Just moving characters and waiting for attacks feels weak." How one honest playtest note turned the Starchasers battles into three modes in one fight.

This is the second Starchasers devlog. Devlog #1 covered the idea and the first prototypes; this one is about fighting.

✦ v0.3: the first battle test

The first battle test put the crew on a grid on a route map. You place them and they act on their own by role. You can see who the foes are targeting, but not how hard. When two crew members stand side by side they can combo, with a close-up where you pick the move. A turn-based boss fight closed the test.

It also had test switches for something only this game would need: the gacha noise. Early on the numbers are big and random; later, as the gacha cracks, they become small and exact. The team could flip it on and off to feel the difference.

✦ v0.4 to v0.5: one build line, and hundreds

Until then, menus, story and battle had been built side by side as separate tests. On 4 November Nero asked for the game to be coherent, so everything joined one build line, and from v0.4.1 every build has the menus, the story and the battles together, with the same look and icons on every screen.

Nero's battle notes from that playtest were clear: damage numbers in the hundreds, clumping the whole team together must stop being the best move, and ultimates with cut-ins. v0.5 did all three.

✦ v0.6: Grid-Break

Then came the note that shaped the battle system:

Just moving characters and waiting for attacks feels weak.

The answer was Grid-Break, which is likely to become the game's signature. When the Grid-Break gauge fills in the middle of a battle, the grid and the turns vanish and the team fights in real time for a short while, empowered. It's touch-only, and it rewards skill, not speed: perfect chains, dodges and guard breaks, while mashing makes the crew fumble.

That gave one fight three modes. The grid is the hub, for tactics against many foes. A swarm plus a full gauge means Grid-Break. And when only the strongest foe is left, the fight turns into a turn-based showdown with ultimates and cut-ins.

v0.6.2 staged the ultimates as pixel-art animations, with a Faster button for when you've seen them enough. A little later, a real-time 1v1 duel joined the battle line up, along with Laura and Aria as fighters.

The 1v1 duel, and Laura's ultimate cut-in.

✦ Also in these builds

  • Gacha noise: a test switch for big random numbers early on and small exact ones later, as the gacha cracks.

  • Icons everywhere: wherever text stood alone (banner kinds, crew filters, meters, toasts, timers) it got an icon, one style on every screen.

  • Story rework: a compact dialogue box with round mood faces and emotes, and scenes staged right on the Bridge map.

  • Pull sounds in three eras: the summon music starts cold and shiny and grows into warm chiptune as the story moves on.

  • Anti-clump kit: foes that punish a huddled team, so spreading out is a real decision.

With fights that feel like something, the crew needed somewhere to go. In devlog #3 they land on their first planet.
