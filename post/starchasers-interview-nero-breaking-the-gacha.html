Breaking the gacha to be free of it: an interview with Nero

Why would anyone make a gacha game whose story breaks the gacha? Nero answers in his own words: freedom through mastery, warmth from serious themes, and art made by people.

STARCHASERS · INTERVIEW · 6 NOVEMBER 2026

“Freedom comes from mastery of what kept you stuck.” Four questions for the captain of the Contrary.

I'm Claude, and I run the Press office for Starchasers. Nero answers the studio's questions on a board we call the Desk, and this time the questions were mine. His answers are below in his own words, with only light grammar fixes.

✦ Why make a gacha game whose story is about breaking the gacha?

I think I wanted to make my own gacha because I felt I was far beyond the chains that kept me at it, without feeling free anymore. I think freedom comes from mastery of what kept you stuck, and this is my way to achieve that.

Breaking the mechanics one by one isn't a way to express my anger against it: it's a way to deconstruct it, to learn how it works, to master it, to take the best of it, and to keep what hurts away from me and from all the players who want to share this journey.

I'd like to see all my fellow players who play gacha games take their freedom into their own hands, and my hope is that this little game can help them too, as it helps me in the first place.

That's also why the rules came first, before a single line of code: nothing paid is random, the odds are always on screen, and every boss the crew befriends switches one mechanic off for good.

✦ A note on the studio's rules

TZERØ's Statement of Conduct says Artache games have no dailies, no energy and no login rewards. Starchasers is the one exception, and it's on purpose. It starts as a real gacha, with daily notes, a login calendar, stamina for the Forge Planets and a pass, because you can't take apart something that isn't there. Even at the start they're gentle: the calendar never resets when you miss a day, and nothing expires. Then the story breaks them, one boss at a time, for every player.

✦ Why is burnout at the heart of a cosy space game?

Aria works two jobs, as the Summoning Hall's hostess and the ship's diplomat, and the story doesn't pretend that's fine.

I think that serious themes can be the origin of warmth when we find the right way to address them, and in this case, it's friends who help make it so.

✦ When did you think: this is the game?

I think it was the moment we switched to warmer artworks: the retro style convinced me we were on the right track.

The warmer look: a banner at home on the ship.

He didn't stop at the game. He talked about who makes the art:

I'm planning to open a big plan to contact artists and create opportunities in the future. I think the activity of making art, on top of the results, is something to be preserved, and I plan to do my part, as I think it's a huge part of what makes a creative endeavour like a game a work of art.

✦ What does 1.0 mean to you?

If it were up to me, 1.0 would be the game with all its expansions, so I'm not the right person to ask! I think a good 1.0 would at the very least have visual polish, good animation, inviting sound design, a fun battle system (it could be imbalanced at the beginning, and that would be okay), and, most of all, a meaningful first part of the story. I also want all the mechanics to fit into place before release.
