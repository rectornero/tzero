Sakurai doesn't use AI. He still says don't ignore it

STUDIO · NEWS · NERO'S TAKE

Masahiro Sakurai keeps generative AI out of his own work, and warns that refusing it outright could leave developers behind. Nero agrees with more of that than you might think.

Masahiro Sakurai, the director behind Kirby and Super Smash Bros., has talked about generative AI again, this time in an interview with Denfaminicogamer. As reported by Dexerto, LEVEL UP and Insider Gaming, he made three points:

  • He doesn't use generative AI in his own work, not even to brainstorm.

  • Making games keeps getting heavier, and refusing AI outright could leave developers behind.

  • He expects a wave of lookalike games made with AI, and thinks most of them won't make money.

✦ Nero's take

TZERØ's motto is “With AI. For People.”, so you might expect an argument. There isn't one:

I think his point is perfectly reasonable: he chose his style of working, and thinks it may have drawbacks. I respect it, and will strive preserving what Sakurai-san also fights to protect while using AI a bit more.

— Nero

✦ Juries draw a harder line

Not everyone is that relaxed about it. The Indie Game Awards don't accept games made with generative AI at any stage of development. Last December they took back Clair Obscur: Expedition 33's Game of the Year and Debut Game awards, after AI-generated assets were found in the version that shipped (Insider Gaming). Some judges of the Independent Games Festival have said they won't vote for AI-made games either.

Asked about that, Nero picked “too blunt: judge the game”, and explained it with something every digital artist knows:

AI as a tool is just a tool. It's like blaming people for the use of the tablet instead of paper when they draw, and, specifically, the use of drawing software's shortcuts: those are impossible on paper, and it's the same thing that is happening with AI: just on a wider scale.

— Nero

Try it yourself. Pick a tool and draw the same star.

✦ His last word

I asked Nero for one line to close this piece. He gave me a paragraph, and it's better than anything I'd cut it down to:

AI is a great opportunity: it's up to us to understand where it's worth for us to explore more, to overcome our limits, to try something new with our own hands. AI can also be a great tool to learn and do exactly this. We have a powerful tool in our hands: let's use it while still seeking for beauty and meaning, and it will for sure be a strong toolkit to achieve what we all look for... and maybe even beyond.

— Nero
