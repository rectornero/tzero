Why this game warmth in a space full of questions

Most gacha games are built to keep you chasing. Starchasers is about a crew that decides to stop the chase, for everyone, and has a lot of fun doing it.

Artache's mission is to walk the gachafication of games backwards, and Starchasers does it from the inside. It looks like the games it's about: banners, pulls, stars, a summoning hall. Then, one boss at a time, the story takes those mechanics apart in front of you. The gacha isn't the target of a joke. It's a metaphor for something everybody knows: the feeling that you're never quite enough.

Freedom comes from mastery of what kept you stuck, and this is my way to achieve that.Nero, in his interview

So the game is sincere first. The crew are Nero's Contrary Quest friends, reimagined as diligent geniuses who are secretly lazy in a genius way, and they set out to save what matters to people: warmth, in a galaxy full of questions.

  • HeartQuiet crew momentsBurnout, limits, worries and far-away family, talked about honestly between friends. Never a lecture.
  • WonderWorlds explored togetherPixel-art vistas, and nine people reacting to them in nine different ways.
  • StrategyReal decisionsGrid tactics built for veteran players, with real-time bursts and big showdowns.
  • The look tells the storyA world that changesPull sounds grow warmer, the corruption fades from the monsters, and small glows tell you something broke. The menus start at their best and stay that way.

How the gacha breaks by discovery

At the start, Starchasers plays like a familiar gacha game, on purpose. The Summoning Hall has banners and a ten-pull, the crew starts at 4★ and can rise to 5★, and the odds board is right there on screen. Then things start to give.

  1. Every boss is a mechanic with a body. When the crew befriends one, that mechanic breaks for good, for every player, and the boss joins the ship.
  2. The odds are always shown, and early on you'll catch the board flickering. That's the first crack.
  3. Pulls sound different over time. The pull music has three eras, from cold and shiny to warm chiptune, and moves along with the story.
  4. 3★ pulls aren't junk. They're the crew's own wishes coming true: they stack and raise your bonds.
Secret 6★ · 1 in 10,000

The Interstellar Pigeon

Every gacha has its legend. Ours is a pigeon. It sits outside pity, it shows up once in ten thousand pulls with the biggest entrance in the game, and in battle it is completely, magnificently useless. It knows. It does not care.

Yes, it's real, and the odds are on the banner like everything else.

  • Nothing paid is randomIf you pay for something, you know exactly what you get.
  • Odds are always shownIn the game, next to the button, not in a menu three screens away.
  • The shop is a secret worldYou unlock it through quests rather than a checkout.

How a fight works three modes, one battle

The first playtest note was blunt: “just moving characters and waiting for attacks feels weak.” So one battle now shifts between three modes, depending on what's in front of you.

Auto-play works everywhere, and Forge runs can be auto-battled. There's also a real-time 1v1 duel for some fights.

Life aboard the Contrary home between worlds

The ship isn't a menu. You walk its decks, the Bridge, the galley, the dock, where the crew are on shift, napping or arguing about tea. There are spots to find, treasures, small goals, and somebody always has something to say.

Spend time with someone and your bond grows: chats with answers that matter to them, chores done together, gifts they actually like, and bond visits, now with pajamas. A strong bond unlocks a small side skill in battle, never a stronger one, so nobody is locked behind a favourite.

This doesn't go in the ledger. This goes on my desk.Laura, given something she loves
Growing the crew

Gear never comes from wishes. You earn it on the Forge Planets, short worlds where clearing early is the challenge and auto-battle is allowed. They pay out gear with main stats and set bonuses, plus the crew's favourite study material: Cheat Sheets, from the Forge Planet called the Crib Archive.

Story

Chapter 1

On the first planet, Weary Reach: three explorable pixel maps, dialogue choices that shape how the crew sees you (heart, wonder, resolve), and visual-novel scenes with expressive character busts.

New roguelite mode

“Inside the Machine”

20 to 30 minute runs where your power-ups are broken gacha mechanics. Branching star-chart routes, a mini summon inside each run, optional challenges, and shareable seed and score codes.

Battle

Grid tactics, then Grid-Break

Grid tactics at the core, plus Grid-Break, our signature. When the gauge fills, the grid vanishes and the team fights in real time on a small lit 2.5D stage. The strongest foe is a turn-based showdown with ultimates and cut-ins. Auto-play everywhere.

Crew

All nine crew playable

Each with skills, an ultimate, a passive and a team combo. Big pixel busts with four moods on every character sheet.

Music

Original soundtrack

Nine tracks made in-house, in a handheld-era tactics-RPG style. Each comes in three warmth eras that cross-fade as the story moves on.

Art

HD splash art

High-definition banners, level-up art, cards and story scenes; true pixel art in the world.

Ship life

A walkable ship

Crew chats and chores, and bond visits (now with pajama costumes).

Menus

Phone-first menus

Big labelled buttons, an in-game Codex book, a bag with “where to find” links, a Settings screen, and a login calendar that never resets on missed days.

First stop: Weary Reach chapter 1

The Contrary's first landing is a tired planet where people keep sitting down halfway and forgetting to stand up. Three tiled maps, walked cell by cell like a classic RPG: the Landing, the Mossway and the Drained Hollow, where the colour has gone out of everything. Fights start when you walk into a nest.

I carry the letters up and down the Mossway. Only lately I keep sitting down halfway and forgetting to stand up.Pell, the letter carrier

The people you meet ask things of you, and every answer leans Heart, Wonder or Resolve. The crew notices, the locals react, and the planet's little epilogue follows the way you leaned.

Questions asked and answered

Is it really a gacha game?

It starts as one, on purpose, so the story can take it apart. Nothing you pay for is random, the odds are always on screen, and every boss the crew befriends removes one gacha mechanic for every player, permanently.

Doesn’t this break TZERØ’s Statement of Conduct?

Yes, on purpose, and it’s the only exception. The Statement of Conduct says no dailies, energy or login rewards. Starchasers starts as a real gacha, with daily notes, a login calendar, stamina for the Forge Planets and a pass, because you can’t take apart something that isn’t there. Even then they’re gentle: the calendar never resets on a missed day and nothing expires. Then the story breaks them, one boss at a time, for every player. Nero explains why in his interview.

What does it cost?

The start is free. After that, each boss arrives as a story chapter you can buy on its own, or all together as one pack. Prices aren't set yet.

Where can I play it?

Android and PC. It's in development, and every test build ships for both.

When does it come out?

No date yet. A new boss chapter is planned every two to three months after launch. We'll say it here first.

Who makes it?

Nero, with Claude and a small studio of AI “offices” (menus, battle, exploration, forge, story, music, art, releases), each with its own job. Nero decides; every question goes to his desk. The art is AI-generated under his art direction, and the soundtrack is composed in code, in-house.

Do I need to know Contrary Quest?

No. The crew comes from Nero's Contrary Quest, but Starchasers is its own story, and everyone is introduced from scratch.
